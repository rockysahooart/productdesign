How I Modernized Securities-Based Lending Without Disrupting Familiar Workflows

I redesigned PNC's application experience to improve form organization, data entry, validation, and underwriting handoffs while preserving familiar user interactions.

Built for high-income individuals and businesses handling large loan requests over $100k.

Demo experience ↗Figma designs ↗GitHub repo ↗View case study ↗

01

Bankers needed to handle more loan applications without more effort

As lending demand grew, the console bankers use to originate securities-based credit had to handle more applications without more effort.

  1. ScalabilityHandle more applications without a matching rise in operational effort.
  2. Workflow fragmentationBorrower, collateral, evaluation and underwriting details sat in disconnected, dense screens, pulling focus from clients.
  3. Submission qualityMissing information, manual checks and late exceptions caused errors, rework and delays.

Compounded by legacy patterns, inconsistent components, accessibility gaps and compliance complexity.

02

I modernized the console without changing how bankers work

I was the sole UX designer for three months, turning these operational challenges into an efficient, connected and usable enterprise experience.

The business asked for modernization, not a new way of working. My redesign keeps the original console's information architecture: the same screens, in the same order, with the same sections, labels and actions where bankers expect them.

03

Bankers now see every open item, and why a step is blocked

What I changed is presentation and behaviour, not the workflow:

  • Application status panel. Shows Requested Loan Amount against Total Maximum Borrowing Power, and every open item grouped by step, each linked to the field where it is completed.
  • Prerequisites with explanations. Next stays disabled until the current step's required items are complete; later steps show "Complete <step> first"; Submit stays disabled until every check is complete; Start Request needs at least one eligible account. The reason always sits beside the disabled control, with links to each missing item. Back and draft saving are never blocked. See Application lifecycle and guidance.
  • Underwriting outcomes. Conditions appear as Action Required · Additional information requested with Respond to condition; a request can also be returned for correction, declined or approved, and keeps its submission history throughout.
