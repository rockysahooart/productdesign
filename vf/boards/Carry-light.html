I was handed a premise from a workshop and three weeks to work in. I scoped it to an MVP, took it through stakeholder review, and handed it to a first build in Playground. A leadership change above me stopped it before release.

My brief was to turn stakeholder direction into something the wider team could review, challenge and build from.

V1 · Week 1 Mapping the first workable flow

I started from stakeholder direction and turned it into a patient journey the team could argue with. There was no interview study behind it. I worked from the team’s premises and the three weeks I had.

Before drawing a screen, I settled three things. Editing would happen in the row and be undoable, because patients kept rewriting questions. Carry would not generate answers. Carry would not record, which came out of a scope conversation with product that moved consent out of the product.

With those three settled, I drew one complete journey. The open marker in the diagram records the recording decision. It is not a gap.

Four things I still could not answer

  • A team premise, carried forward as an assumption.
  • Upload, transcription and matching all take real time.
  • Whether an excerpt can point at a moment in the audio. Drawn as if it could, flagged as a question for engineering.
  • It constrained more than it first looked.

I logged all four in Jira as open questions. Two of them were somebody else’s to answer.

Patient rights & protections

I designed Carry so patients could return to their own care conversations without giving up control of the experience.

The right in play is a patient’s access to their own health information, and their say in how it is handled. Carry is built on material the patient supplies: they choose whether to upload a recording at all, and declining had to be a real option rather than a dead end, so the upload state is designed to be refused. Questions are editable in the row and every suggestion is undoable from the moment it lands, which keeps the record theirs to correct rather than a transcript they are handed.

I treated HIPAA and PHI review as a design gate before the first iteration. The patient-facing copy, what each screen exposed, and the behaviour of the upload, transcription and matching states went through that review before V1 moved into iteration, and the same questions came back on the project calls as states were added. That is where the recording question surfaced: rather than design around consent, the scope conversation moved it out of the product.

I ruled out generated clinical answers. A matched answer is a quotation from the recording, badged, with a route back to the moment it came from, and where nothing in the recording answers a question clearly enough to quote, the question stays visibly unanswered. That decision is a patient-protection decision before it is a product one: a patient cannot check a summary against what was said in the room, and a wrong answer about a result can send someone into months of surveillance.

Recording stayed outside the MVP. Consent and privacy complexity surfaced in a scope conversation with product, and the resolution was to move consent out of the product rather than design around it. The copy carries the same intent: Carry asks for file access in its own words before the picker opens and says only audio files will be shown, so the request is legible and the exposure is no wider than the task.

Designed in support of patient access to their information (45 CFR §164.524), requests for confidential communications and restrictions (§164.522), reasonable safeguards (§164.530(c)), and the minimum necessary standard (§164.502(b), §164.514(d)). These informed the decisions above; no interface on its own establishes compliance.

One thing I deferred rather than solved

I assumed that patients want to prepare, that they will record their own visit, and that they will come back and read it. None of those assumptions was tested before I drew. If I had the time again, I would run that test first.

What engineering received

  • The patient flow on both sides of the appointment, connected end to end
  • Component states for upload, transcription and matching, including every failure
  • Conditional behaviour: what is kept after each failure, and what a retry does
  • The unanswered case, where a question has no quotable answer, as a designed state
  • Permission and picker copy, written as a requirement rather than a suggestion
  • The component set: one colour, one ink ramp, one row, with heights and type measured
  • The open dependencies, left visible on the flow and in the ticket instead of quietly resolved

Reconstructed from the documented upload flow and the failure states in Screens and states.

Acceptance criteria

Changing a question before the visit

Reconstructed from the documented editing flow and the prepare screen.

Story
A patient writes a question, thinks better of the wording, and rewrites it, often more than once, which is the behaviour the pilot kept showing.
Acceptance criteria
  • Editing happens in the row itself.
  • Adding a suggested question is undoable from the moment it lands.
  • The row grows with the question, so a long one is never clipped.
Who it supported
The two other designers, who built screens from the row once it was in the component set.

Terms used in this project

PatientX
The patient experience this client already shipped. It owned sign-in, appointments and navigation, and Carry had to fit inside it without changing any of it.
Carry
The mobile-only system I designed: it connects the questions a patient writes before an appointment with what they need to remember afterward.
Preparation checklist
The logistics list a patient works through before a visit. In V3 it sat above the questions; testing moved it below them.
Suggested questions
Prompts Carry offers a patient who is less sure what to ask. Adding one is undoable from the moment it lands.
Transcription
Turning the recording a patient uploaded into text. It is one of the three steps that can fail, and it fails separately from the upload.
Matching
The step that ties a question the patient wrote to the moment in the recording that answers it.
Matched answer
What matching produces: a quotation from the recording, badged, with a route back to the moment it came from. Where nothing is quotable, the question stays visibly unanswered.
Maze
The unmoderated testing tool run in parallel with the onsite sessions during the workshop week.
Playground
The environment where the first build of the MVP started after the workshop.
